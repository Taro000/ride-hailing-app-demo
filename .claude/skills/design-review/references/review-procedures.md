## Rules for every review

- Work from the artifacts. What is not written down does not exist; when it matters, that is a finding.
- Every activity has a result: what you computed, traced or walked, and what it showed. Record the activities that found nothing too.
- Quote the evidence with IDs and numbers. Compute with a script when there is arithmetic.
- Address each finding to the skill that owns its cause, and give it a severity as `artifacts.md` defines.
- Check the stage against its own exit criteria as well; an unmet exit criterion is a finding, unless `scripts/check.py` checks it. In a diff review, check them for the items in scope and for the hand-offs that changed.
- Check each point at the depth its stage decides it ("Decision depth" in `conventions.md`): the stage's full-depth kinds, and the points that rate high, in full; a promise it decides as bounds, as a promise that later stages can keep; a point it hands off, only as a hand-off (E7). A detail handed off with its stage and trigger is not missing: raise no finding about its absence, in any activity.
- Run `scripts/check.py` (without `--skill`) before the activities. Raise no finding about what it checks ("Scripts" in `conventions.md`): it reports each problem to the stage that owns the file, every time a stage finishes. Under "Open gaps", name the stages that still have problems in the items in scope, with their number, so that the reader knows the review did not cover them.
- The first review of a stage is a full review: every activity, for the whole stage. Every later review is a diff review, unless the user asks for a full one or the last review's commit cannot be found (SKILL.md, "Inputs").

### Diff reviews

A diff review checks what changed since the last review of the stage, and what a change can break. The last review covered the rest.

1. Find the changes. The base is the last review's commit: the last commit whose first line starts with this skill's name and that touched this report (`git log -1 --format=%h --grep='^design-review' -- <report>`). A review that resumes one waiting for answers keeps instead the base that its report's header names (SKILL.md, "Inputs"). List and read what changed after it under `docs/quality/` and `docs/design/`, as "Re-running a stage" in `conventions.md` describes. The changes that put items in scope are:
   - an item of the stage under review that is new, changed or retired, the hand-offs it wrote included, and a changed part of its report (a view, a walkthrough, a flow, a budget, a state diagram, a decision table) or of a spec;
   - a changed input of the stage: an item of an earlier stage or of the quality chain, or an entry of `design-context.yaml`, unless the change only rewords it without changing its meaning.

   Here and in the columns below, "changed" includes new and retired. Findings and assumptions that changed, and changes to the items of a later stage, put nothing in scope: E1 and E3 read them.
2. Find the connected items. An item of the stage under review is connected to a change when it names a changed item, or a changed item names it, in `premises`, `depends_on` or a trace field: any field that refers to other items by ID, such as `addresses`, `functions`, `drivers`, `decisions`, `provider`, `consumers`, `owner`, `realizes` or `sli_bindings`. A part of the report that shows a changed item is connected to it. One step is enough: the activities follow a change further where they need to.
3. The items in scope are the changed and connected items of the stage under review. The open gaps of the last review are in scope too: check what can be checked now, and list the rest again.
4. Do each activity for what its "In a diff review" column names, and record an activity with nothing there as "No change in scope".
5. Raise a finding only about what a change brought: a problem in a changed item, or one that a change causes in another item, whose evidence then names that change. Do not raise a new finding about what has not changed, even when you notice one: the last review covered it. A problem that looks critical there is no finding either; note it in one line under "Open gaps", for the user to decide on a full review. An open finding of an earlier review that still holds is listed again in the report, not raised again (E1).

## Stage 2: architecture

| # | Activity | Result to record | In a diff review |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Walk each primary driver's scenario through the design: where the stimulus enters, which elements and decisions shape the response, whether the response can meet the measure, judging each value that is handed off by its bounds | Per driver: risks, non-risks, sensitive points, trade-offs | The primary drivers that changed or became primary, and those that a changed decision addresses or a changed element carries |
| A2 | Walk each CUJ, step by step, through the elements and their interactions | Per step: the elements, the interactions, any gap | The CUJs that changed, and the steps that a changed element or interaction takes part in |
| A3 | Answer four questions, with evidence: Is the design fit for its use (the CUJs and the drivers)? Will it hold up over its life and its planned changes (the change scenarios, the debts)? Can this team build and run it by the deadline and within the budget, and is it worth its cost? Is it clear to those who will build, use and maintain it? | One short paragraph each | Each question that a change bears on, for the changed part: use (changed drivers, CUJs and decisions), life (changed change scenarios, debts and decisions), cost (a changed design context, changed costs and elements), clarity (the changed items) |
| A4 | Check every decision: real options, a rationale tied to drivers, rejected options with reasons, gains and costs, constraints respected | The decisions that fall short, and how | The decisions in scope |
| A5 | Check the four views (context, runtime, deployment, information) against the consistency rules of stage 2: every element in each view its kind belongs in, with one ID and name in all of them (components in runtime and deployment; data stores in runtime, deployment and information; external systems in context and runtime); every connector between elements that exist, and every data flow along a connector; every component on a node | Each rule: holds, or where it fails | The views that changed or show a changed element |
| A6 | Allocation: `scripts/check.py` checks that every FR is allocated to a component, every driver to elements, and every component to a team when teams are given; the review does not repeat it | "Checked by the script" | "Checked by the script" |
| A7 | Check the threat model: every flow that crosses a trust boundary, walked for the six kinds of threat | Flows or threats not covered | The flows across a trust boundary that changed or pass through a changed element, and, for a changed security driver, the flows that carry its assets |
| A8 | Check the eight design concerns: each settled by decisions, with what stage 2 leaves of it named with the later stage that decides it and the trigger, or set aside with a reason that holds | Concerns with a weak reason; parts of a concern that no stage takes up | The concerns that a changed decision settles, those whose part left to a later stage a change touches, and those set aside with a reason that a change touches |
| A9 | Check deferrals: each deferral of a point of stage 2's full-depth kinds, or of one that rates high, is a hand-off (`scripts/check.py` checks that each debt has its condition to repay and each hand-off its trigger) | Deferrals that are not hand-offs | The changed decisions, and the changed hand-offs that stage 2 wrote |
| A10 | Check the fit with the teams: components that most change scenarios make two teams change together | The pairs found | The components whose team, boundary or change scenarios changed |
| A11 | Check the design inputs and platforms: every DIN in the quality chain's `context.yaml` is a technology constraint in `design-context.yaml`, or set aside with a reason in stage 1's report; the platforms (operating systems, browsers and their versions, native or web) are decided, by a technology constraint or a stage 2 decision, for every kind of device the contexts of use name | DINs neither taken in nor set aside; kinds of device with no platform decided | The DINs and contexts of use that changed, and the platform decisions that changed |

`scripts/check.py` checks the allocation that A6 names, that every debt has its condition to repay, and that every hand-off has its trigger.

A DIN that stage 1 neither took in nor set aside is a finding addressed to `requirements-to-architecture-drivers`. A platform that nothing decides is a finding addressed to `architecture-drivers-to-architecture`.

How the code is organized (repositories, directories, dependency rules) is not a view of stage 2: P2 and P3 check it in stage 2b. A decision or an element of stage 2 that chooses what stage 2b decides (a framework or a library, the layout of the code, a test layer, a convention inside the code) fails one of stage 2's exit criteria: a finding addressed to `architecture-drivers-to-architecture`.

## Stage 2b: technical policy

| # | Activity | Result to record | In a diff review |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Check the stacks and the shared tools: each kind of component has one stack, and every component of the kind uses it; each stack keeps the technology constraints and the runtime and platform that stage 2 decided; it fits the team's skills and the drivers it touches (the deadline, the cost, the change scenarios), or the gap is a business decision under "Decisions" or a DAS of kind `decision`; the package manager, the build, the tool that changes database schemas and how each component runs locally are decided, with where the local copy of a service differs from the real one | Stacks that break a constraint, differ within a kind, or need skills that nobody decided to acquire; shared tools left undecided | The stacks and shared tools that changed, and those of the components whose kind, runtime, platform or team changed |
| P2 | Compare the repositories with stage 2's deployment and the teams: every component that stage 2 deploys on its own can be built, tested and released on its own from its repository; no repository makes two teams change it together for most change scenarios, and none splits what one team changes together; the code structure in the report places every component in its directory, with its owning team when the design context names several | Mismatches between the repositories and the units of deployment or the teams; components missing from the code structure | The repositories that changed, and those that hold a component whose deployment, node or team changed |
| P3 | Check the dependency rules: each names the paths or packages it constrains and a tool and pipeline step that fails on a breach, in a form that the tool can check; together they hold the boundaries that stage 2's decisions rely on (between components, around each external system's adapter, between the business rules and the frameworks); a rule that imports cannot show, such as which module writes a table, names a check that can | Rules that no tool can check; boundaries that no rule holds | The dependency rules that changed, and the boundaries of the decisions and elements that changed |
| P4 | Check the test strategy: every external system that a component calls has a test double and its seam, or a reason for none; each double can produce the outcomes the design handles (errors, timeouts, late answers); the clock and IDs can be replaced wherever a driver or an acceptance criterion depends on time or IDs; each layer says what it covers, what it replaces and where it runs; no environment gets a copy of personal or payment data | Gaps | The layers, doubles and environments that changed, and the external systems and drivers that changed |
| P5 | Check the gate bindings and the audit bindings: each gate binding realizes its NFR's `where`, check and threshold without changing them (a gate the quality chain puts in CI is not moved to a release check, and no threshold is loosened); each step, layer, environment, double and tool it names exists in the policies; each audit binding holds its audit where its cadence or deadline is kept, with where its record lives; neither copies the NFR's threshold, criterion or cadence | Bindings that change what a gate requires or name what does not exist; audits that nothing holds to their cadence | The gates and audits that changed, the bindings that changed, and the bindings that name a step, layer, environment or double that changed |
| P6 | Check the pipeline and the conventions inside the code: every step says when it runs and what it blocks, and the static checks block a merge; deployment builds once and rolls back within stage 2's deployment; the conventions say how errors are handled and passed on, how logs are written, where settings and secrets come from, and where the texts for people live; none fixes a convention that interfaces show to their consumers, which is stage 3's | Gaps; conventions that overlap stage 3's | The steps and conventions that changed |
| P7 | Check the spec rules: every kind of interface that stage 2's context and runtime views show (with an external system, called from the users' devices, used by another team, between this system's own elements) has a rule; a spec is written by hand when the other side is implemented apart from the code, and generated from the code otherwise, unless the policy records why not; each rule names its tool and the check that keeps the code and the spec together | Kinds of interface with no rule; generated specs for interfaces that another party implements; rules with no check | The spec rules that changed, and the kinds of interface of the connectors, external systems and teams that changed |
| P8 | Check the feedback from implementation: coding agents are pointed to the design and the policies before they change code; they run the checks of the pipeline's check step before they finish; a finding reaches the stage that owns the item it concerns, this stage for a policy, and the pipeline checks the finding files | Gaps | The implementation-feedback policy, when it changed |

`scripts/check.py` checks that every scope has one active policy, that every component is in exactly one stack entry and one repository, that every dependency rule names its check, that every gate and audit of the quality chain is bound in a policy or replaced by a request to the quality chain, and that each binding names an NFR of the right kind.

A gate or an audit that no design within the constraints can realize as the quality chain defines it is a request to the quality chain, not a finding; a policy that adds a check on a quality requirement that no gate asks for is found by E8. A stack or a repository split that only an earlier stage can fix (a technology constraint that rules out every option the drivers need, a deployment that splits what one team must change together) is a finding addressed to the stage that decided it.

## Stage 3: high-level design

| # | Activity | Result to record | In a diff review |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | Trace every acceptance criterion of every allocated FR through the IFCs and flows | The criteria that cannot be traced | The criteria of the FRs that changed, and of the FRs that a changed IFC or flow serves |
| B2 | Recompute every latency budget with a script: the normal path, counting only the time that the SLI's binding observes, with each serial hop at its share of the SLO's tail; each failure path's duration and the number of valid events that take it, against the error budget volume | The normal path's sum against the threshold; each failure path's share of the error budget | The budgets whose NFR, SLI or flow changed, or whose normal or failure path uses a changed IFC |
| B3 | Check every IFC's error contract: every case has a signal and says what the caller does; retries only on idempotent operations; a timeout on a call that changes something is an unknown outcome, settled with the same party before any other is tried; "nothing happened" is said only when every party asked has confirmed it | Gaps; unsafe retries; unknown outcomes reported as failures | The IFCs in scope, and the flows that use a changed one |
| B4 | Check every timeout and wait on the paths of time SLOs and of money: above its dependency's slowest healthy response, within the caller's remaining time, and below the caller's own timeout with settlement included; no SLO threshold used as a timeout. On every other call between elements, check only that the IFC's `timing` states the bounds and that the values are handed off to stage 4 | Timeouts that cut healthy calls; callers that give up on running work; other calls with no bounds or no hand-off | The timeouts on every call path that includes a changed IFC, from the first caller to the last dependency, and the changed hand-offs of timeouts |
| B5 | Check every event IFC: delivery guarantee, duplicate handling, ordering key, versioning | Gaps | The event IFCs in scope |
| B6 | Check every DAT: one writer; every reader goes through the owner's interface, a copy fed by the owner's events, or a recorded read-only decision; a consistency rule, a retention period, a classification | Second writers; unrecorded readers; missing rules | The DATs in scope, and the DATs that a changed IFC or element reads or writes |
| B7 | Check the SLI bindings: every SLI of the quality chain has an `sli_bindings` entry on an IFC or a DAT; the bound events are the SLI's unit event, observed at its vantage, from the environment and traffic its `applicability` names and where its `lifecycle` says it is measured; the fields carry what its VALID and GOOD definitions, exclusions and counting rules need; the method names a source that exists (the SLI's `existing_source`, or one the design provides); nothing in the binding changes what the SLI counts | SLIs without a binding; bindings that observe another event or from another vantage, or count by other rules; missing fields | The SLIs that changed, and the bindings on the IFCs and DATs in scope |
| B8 | Check that every IFC and every spec follows the shared conventions, and that the shared conventions decide only what interfaces show to their consumers: none decides how the code raises and passes on errors, writes logs or reads settings and secrets, or where the texts for people live, which the technical policy's pipeline-and-code policy holds | Deviations; shared conventions that overlap the technical policy | The IFCs and specs in scope; all of them when a shared convention changed; the shared conventions, when a shared convention or the pipeline-and-code policy changed |
| B9 | Check the state diagrams across elements: every entity whose life spans several elements and that holds money, a booking or an unknown outcome, or whose states rate high, has one; in each, every state has a way out, timeouts and cancellations are present, and no state that money, goods or a booking can still leave is final | Entities that need a diagram and have none; dead ends; final states that hide an unknown outcome | The state diagrams that changed or show a changed IFC or element, and the entities in scope that need one |
| B10 | Compare what each IFC says about requests, responses and errors (fields, headers, the values that come with an error, what the caller does) with the schemas and descriptions of its spec. Check that every HTTP or event interface follows the entry of the specs policy that covers its kind: a spec under `ifc/specs/` for the kinds written by hand, and only for those; a schema handed off to implementation for the kinds generated from the code, which is no gap; the owner's document linked for the kinds whose other side owns it, with the schema handed off too when a component of this system serves the interface | Mismatches; interfaces whose spec does not follow the specs policy; interfaces that no entry covers | The IFCs and specs that changed, the changed hand-offs of schemas, and the IFCs whose kind a changed entry of the specs policy covers |

An SLI that no design within the constraints can bind as defined (its unit event cannot be seen from its vantage, or a counting rule is ambiguous) is a request to the quality chain, not a finding.

An interface whose spec does not follow its entry of the specs policy, and a shared convention that decides a convention inside the code, are findings addressed to `architecture-to-high-level-design`. An interface that no entry covers, or whose entry goes against the policy's own rule (another party builds against an interface whose spec the policy says is generated from the code), is a finding addressed to `architecture-to-technical-policy`.

`scripts/check.py` checks that every SLI is bound or names, in the SLI bindings table, an open or answered request to the quality chain that targets it, that every allocated FR is carried by an interface, that every HTTP or event interface that a component provides has a spec or a hand-off of its schema to implementation, and that each spec has the operations of its IFC, a response for each error status the IFC gives an operation, and each error code the IFC names.

## Stage 4: detailed design

| # | Activity | Result to record | In a diff review |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Check the allocation: every acceptance criterion of the component goes to a module; each operation the component calls on an IFC it consumes has a module that calls it; every event and field that the `sli_bindings` on its IFCs and DATs need it to produce goes to a module | Gaps | The modules in scope, and the criteria, operations and SLI bindings that changed |
| C2 | Check every module's depth: full when it moves money, settles an unknown outcome, shares state with work that runs at the same time, applies rules with several conditions or migrates data, or when it would rate high at bounds depth; bounds otherwise. A module of those kinds is at bounds depth only by a deferral that its `depth_reason` cites. Then check its parts: at full depth, one responsibility, contracts with errors, behaviour, data structures, a verification approach; at bounds depth, only its responsibility, the summary of its contract with its errors, its allocation and its verification approach | Modules of those kinds at bounds depth with no business decision to defer them; modules at full depth with no reason that holds; missing parts | The modules in scope |
| C3 | Check the state diagram of every module at full depth: every event defined in every state; a final state or a named wait reachable from each state; every timeout on a call that changes something leads to a state that settles it | Undefined events; dead ends; timeouts that skip settlement | The state diagrams that changed or belong to a module in scope |
| C4 | Check every decision table of the modules at full depth: count the combinations; every one covered once | Missing or overlapping rows | The decision tables that changed or belong to a module in scope |
| C5 | Check the module dependencies: direction toward stable modules; every dependency within the dependency rules of the technical policy, and every module in the directory that its rules give; no rule of the component's own | Inversions; dependencies that a rule of the policy forbids; modules outside the places the rules give; boundaries between modules that no rule holds | The dependencies to and from the changed modules, and all of them when the repository policy changed |
| C6 | Check concurrency and idempotency: shared state; what must be atomic; money movement that stays idempotent with a duplicate arriving while the first request runs, with a key reused for a different request, and with failover after an unknown outcome | Races; double effects | The changed modules, and the modules that share state with them |
| C7 | Check the data of the modules at full depth: every invariant of stored data they rely on is kept by a constraint, an index, a transaction, a conditional update or the rights of a database role; the constraints match the contracts; migrations keep the running version working; every table another component reads directly is designed in full | Invariants that nothing keeps; mismatches; tables read from outside that are not designed | The invariants and tables that changed, and those that a changed module at full depth relies on |
| C8 | Check that the design parameters fit the stage 3 budgets and timeouts; that the component sets no timeout or wait that an IFC's `timing` holds, and refers to those by IFC ID; and that each timeout that stage 3 handed off to the component, for a call to another element, is set within the bounds in the IFC's `timing` by a module at full depth, or passed on to implementation with them | Overruns; timeouts or waits set in the component that an IFC holds; handed-off timeouts outside their bounds | The changed parameters, those bound by an IFC `timing` or a budget that changed, and the timeouts handed off to the component |
| C9 | Check verifiability: every criterion and contract has a verification approach | Items that cannot be verified | The modules in scope, and the criteria that changed |
| C10 | Check the component against the shared conventions of stage 3 (errors, IDs, time, money, the request ID and the log fields that SLIs need) and the technical policies: it is built on the stack of its kind, and no decision of stage 4 chooses a framework or a main library, or a library that the policy did not hand to it; its error handling and logs keep the conventions inside the code; its verification approaches use the policy's test layers, doubles and environments | Deviations | The modules in scope; all of them when a shared convention or a technical policy changed |

`scripts/check.py` checks that the functions and drivers of each module are among its component's, that every function of the component is in a module, that each operation of an IFC the component provides is realized by exactly one of its modules and no operation by two, that every decision of the detailed design is used by a module, and that the module dependencies form no cycle.

A module at bounds depth is looked at only for its responsibility, the summary of its contract, its allocation, its verification approach and its place among the module dependencies (C1, C2, C5, C9, C10). Its inside is implementation's: no state, decision table, table or parameter missing from it is a finding, except a table another component reads directly, which C7 checks whichever module writes it.

A dependency that a rule of the repository policy forbids, a module outside the places its rules give, a framework or a library that stage 4 chose without a hand-off from stage 2b, and a check at a layer that the policy lacks without a finding addressed to stage 2b, are findings addressed to `high-level-design-to-detailed-design`. A budget that only a load test can check needs no layer of the policy: it names the request to the quality chain for the gate instead. A boundary between modules that no rule holds and that a mistake in the code would cross at a cost, a rule that blocks every reasonable split, and a stack that cannot do what a module needs, are findings addressed to `architecture-to-technical-policy`.

## Every stage

Do these in every review, after the stage's own activities. They check what the protocol in `conventions.md` leaves in the items: findings, premises, assumptions, decisions, traces, the points that the quality chain leaves for the design to confirm, hand-offs, and what the design leaves to the quality chain.

| # | Activity | Result to record | In a diff review |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Read every finding in `fnd/`, whatever its status, that targets items in scope or whose `raised_in` is this report's path: an accepted one has its change in the items; a rejected one is not raised again unless its inputs changed; an open one that still holds is not written twice | Accepted findings whose change is missing; the findings not raised again, and why | The findings that are new or whose status changed, and those whose targets are in scope. The other open findings raised at this path still hold: list them again without checking them |
| E2 | Check the premises: every item in scope that relies on an assumption (as its text or its `assumptions` shows) lists it in `premises`, and an item that rests on a QAS that a design question answered cites the answer in `basis_note`; every item in scope that would change with an open or answered request to the quality chain lists the request in `premises` (or the QAS whose answer it carries), and one that follows an answered request cites the answer | Items with a missing premise | The items in scope |
| E3 | Re-rate every open DAS and QAS that the items in scope or the findings depend on, as `conventions.md` describes ("Draft first, ask afterwards"): breadth from the structure, now that later stages may depend on it too, and severity from the harm if it is wrong. Update each DAS's `breadth`, `severity` and `rating_note` in place; a QAS file is never written, so its ratings show only in the report. Ask in this run about each one that now rates high on either scale | Per assumption: its ratings now; those high on either scale, and the question that asked each, or the finding raised when no answer could be had | The open DAS and QAS whose file changed, and those that a changed item of any stage, later stages included, names in `premises` |
| E4 | Check the business decisions in the items in scope (provisional design parameters; decisions that accept a risk, a cost in money or contracts, a weaker driver or a debt; deferrals of points of the stage's full-depth kinds, or of points that rate high; the stage's rejections of findings that leave a risk in place): each was asked and is under "Decisions" in the stage's report, or is a DAS of `kind: decision` that the items depending on it name in `premises`. Every decision lists its costs; that alone does not make it a business decision, nor does handing off any other point | Business decisions that nobody decided and no DAS records | The items in scope, the changed hand-offs that the stage wrote, and the findings that the stage rejected since the last review |
| E5 | Look for quality-chain statements copied into design items instead of traced by ID: the wording of a statement, an acceptance criterion or an SLI definition, or the value of a shared fact (CON, EXT, OPF), written as the item's own text | The copies found | The changed items, and the items that trace to a changed quality-chain item |
| E6 | Check the points that quality-chain items leave for the design to confirm (in their `basis_note`), which stage 1 turns into drivers, constraints, questions, DAS or hand-offs to the stage that designs the part: every point addressed to the stage under review or an earlier one is confirmed by the stage it is addressed to, cited in the `basis_note` of the item that confirms it, passed on to a later stage, or raised as a request to the quality chain | Points neither confirmed, passed on nor raised, with the stage each is addressed to | The points that are new or changed (in a quality-chain item's `basis_note`, or in a hand-off), and those whose confirming item changed |
| E7 | Check the hand-offs that the stage under review wrote (for stage 4, those of the component): none hands off a point of the stage's full-depth kinds, or one that rates high, unless its deferral is a business decision, which E4 checks; the bounds of each can hold together | Points of the stage's full-depth kinds, or rated high, handed off without a business decision; bounds that cannot hold together | The new and changed hand-offs that the stage wrote |
| E8 | Look for design items that decide or change what the quality chain owns (`conventions.md`, "The quality chain"): a gate (a check that the work must pass on a quality requirement before it is merged, shown, released or called done), a target or a threshold that the system's quality must reach, an acceptance criterion or another requirement written as the design's own, or a change to what an SLI counts, made a decision, a parameter, a verification or a criterion of the design | The items found, and the request each one should be | The changed items |

- E1: An open finding that no longer holds is said so in the result, for the addressed stage to settle; the findings table lists it while it is open. To tell whether a rejected finding's inputs changed, diff the items it names since the commit that rejected it (`git log -1 --format=%h -- docs/design/fnd/FND-07.yaml`, then `git diff <commit> -- <files>`). `scripts/check.py` checks that an accepted finding is cited in the `basis_note` of an item.
- E2: `scripts/check.py` checks the basis against the premises (an open DAS, or an open QAS that `basis_note` does not cite, makes an item `assumed`, and an `assumed` item names one) and that every ID in `premises` exists. An item still marked `assumed` because its stage has not run since its assumptions were answered is the check's business, not a finding: the stage applies the answers when it runs ("Assumptions" in `conventions.md`).
- E3: An assumption whose breadth or severity is high after re-rating is asked in this run, whichever stage wrote it ("Assumptions" in `conventions.md`): record the question and its answer under "Decisions", write the answer in the DAS (`answer`, `answered_in`) and set its status; for a QAS, write a request to the quality chain that carries the answer, with `stage` set to this review ("Assumptions" in `conventions.md`). If the answer overturns it, tell the user to re-run the stage that wrote it and the stages whose items depend on it. Only when the user cannot answer it, write a finding instead: for a DAS, addressed to the stage that wrote it (its `stage`), suggesting that it ask on its next run; for a QAS, addressed to the stage under review. A question written down because nobody could answer during the run ("Draft first, ask afterwards", step 7, in `conventions.md`) waits for its answer instead. Rate likewise the impact of each open request to the quality chain that the items in scope depend on, and ask the ones whose impact is now high ("Requests to the quality chain" in `conventions.md`).
- E5: A short label next to the ID is not a copy, nor is a number used in a calculation with its source. A copy that already differs from its source is a contradiction (major); one that still matches is minor.
- E6: A point addressed to the stage under review, or to an earlier design stage, that it neither confirmed, passed on nor raised as a request is a finding addressed to that stage. A point that stage 1 turned into none of a driver, a constraint, a question, a DAS or a hand-off is a finding addressed to `requirements-to-architecture-drivers`. A point addressed to a later stage is not a finding yet.
- E7: A detail handed off with its stage and trigger is not missing, in this activity or any other, until the stage it is addressed to has run past its trigger without deciding it or passing it on; that is then a finding addressed to that stage. A hand-off whose bounds cannot hold together is a finding addressed to the stage that set them. A point of the full-depth kinds, or one that rates high, that a stage handed off without a business decision is a finding addressed to that stage. `scripts/check.py` checks that each hand-off names a valid addressee, its component when it goes to stage 4 or to implementation, a trigger, and items that exist.
- E8: Each item found is a finding addressed to the stage that wrote it, major, since no one who owns the requirements decided it: a gate before a demo that the design wrote into a decision's parameters is one. Its suggestion is to take the gate, the level or the change out of the item, and to write a request to the quality chain for it when the design still needs it. A latency budget that splits an SLO's threshold, a parameter that tunes a mechanism (a breaker's error threshold, a timeout), and how a part is verified, a check in CI that the code keeps a decision of the design included, are design, unless they make a quality requirement a condition of a merge, a demo, a release or done, or set a level that the system's quality must reach.

## Risks, non-risks, sensitive points and trade-offs

- A risk is a decision, or a missing one, that may keep a driver's response from meeting its measure.
- A non-risk is a decision that was checked and holds, with the reason. Recording non-risks shows what was checked.
- A sensitive point is a property the response depends on strongly: a threshold, a timeout, a number of instances.
- A trade-off is a property that helps one driver and hurts another.

Every risk becomes a finding. A sensitive point that names no design parameter, or whose value is neither set in its stage nor handed off with its stage and trigger, and a trade-off recorded on only one of its drivers, are findings too: minor, unless they hide a risk. In a diff review, this holds for what a change brings in ("Diff reviews", step 5).
